## Public interface copy

- Each visible heading, sentence, icon and container must help readers identify a subject, understand evidence, or take an action.
- Use one descriptive page title. Do not add a kicker or subtitle that restates it, introduces the section generically, or advertises usefulness.
- Preserve meaningful dates, sources, sample scope, methodology, uncertainty and accessible labels. Removing all descriptions mechanically is not simplification.
- In the weekly edition, lead with the concrete subject, proposed effects and actual decision. Keep citations and detailed votes available through progressive disclosure.
- Use spacing, readable typography and clear article separators. Avoid decorative badges, section letters, repeated numbers, gratuitous animations and nested cards.
- The default weekly edition is complete. Saved interests never silently hide stories; filtering requires an explicit URL.
- Generated summaries must distinguish proposed changes from established decisions. Do not manufacture effective dates, affected groups or citizen actions to fill UI slots.
